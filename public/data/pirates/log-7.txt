<strong>So this is what I can manage to remember about the last thrilling Pirate Game.</strong> I may be light on details and my story-telling prowess is being held hostage by my failure of memory in just what exactly happened when and maybe even to whom. But nevertheless, we endeavor to persevere!

I have impressions of what happened to me so I will try and write that with some coherence.

We got caught up in some adventure regarding the Witt Brothers who live at Witts End: Nit, Half, Vic, and Dimm. Yeah, how does it feel Tim?

The Client, Vic Wit, hired the crew to take him down to Mourningwood to find a mausoleum with the inscription “Here lies the cleric, Apton, priest, singer of songs, died tragically before his wedding." He wants something tucked away inside the stone crypt and for the gang to take it back up shore, no questions asked.

His good cold cash was good enough for Captain Berand van der Meer, so down south we went, following the shoreline past the last Elysian town called Strathmore and on down to Mourningwood.

Mourningwood is mainly a big forest, but on the coastline is a good size cemetery/final resting place for many unfortunate souls. Supposedly many of these unfortunates lived sad lives darkened by unrequited feelings of love and/or accomplishment, and so went to their lonely graves with bitter feelings of unsatisfied accomplishments and failed romance and relationships.

Also supposedly, small children everywhere know the legend that one...ONE night of every year, the dead of the Mourningwood Cemetery are stirred to rise, fully erect in their broken bodies, to draw other dumbheads who are stupid enough to be there to their own very new doom because what is even the point of a cursed cemetary if you can't fill it with more dead people 

Fortunately, the only possible victims for the undead beings are those who feel a sense of still-wanting-to accomplish-something-in-life. Now the Witts, who mostly seem to live a day-to-day existence, might actually be safe on that score. But I've never met a PC who didn't feel he wanted to do something with his unspent XP.

Somehow, Mourningwood did not cause the blood to rise actively in anyone's head and brain to remember the details of this particular legend before it became too late. Also, there is no port this far south, jdespite the potential benefits for any logging interests trying to monetize the stiff timbers of the Mourningwood Forest. The Wicked Hart must anchor offshore with the crew using a small boat to dock on land.

But I am getting ahead of myself. We found the mausoleum and opened it up to see a barrel therin.  There were also component parts to build a handy cart to transport the barrel to the shoreline to load it on board the ship.  The large barrel is 3/4 full of what smells like vinegar, and Morgan thought she heard a moan from inside, though no one else did.

Inquisitive Jonathon secretly determined the barrel has magickal protections against detect/sense, although this did not stop plucky Jon from being able to determine that there is something magickal inside!!!

The "facts" get real dodgey from here on.

It turns out that Vic Witt had made a deal with the Captain of the Illithid ship, the Broken Wind. The barrel was probably supposed to be kept safe, transported south, and then loaded on to the Broken Wind at a set date which was roughly Real Soon Now. Unfortunately, nobody makes a fool toadie out of Victor Von Witt, and he realized the barrel must be the secret to Great Power worth way hecka more than whatever prize purse he was paid in good faith by the Illithid. Besides, Illithids are evil and dark and sinister and all sorts of things bad and so that guy was just acting for it to be betrayed! Vic paid Capt. Barend the rich monies to maintain his loyalty that weren't enough to assure Vic's own. Is it any surprise that the crew didn't figure any of this was on the up and up and deserved more than a little poking about?

Somewhere around here, a little kid with a red balloon and pulling a small wagon walked by the whole scene, telling everyone, "The ghosts will get you if you don't watch out!" Palms struck foreheads everywhere as the reality of our predicament came clear.

Being students of the classics, we probably all tried to plug our ears with wax so we could not hear the plaintive siren calls of doom as night fell.  This didn't help a whole lot though, and the dead got seriously out of hand.

Some of the dead had names and could turn into sharks and octopi or whatever you're afraid of or something like that, who knows??? I could I swear Jonathon saw clowns...scary, terrifying clowns!!!

People would get possessed and walk into the sea to drown their sorrows by drowning. I have vague recollections of Jonathon trying to send his spirit out to help others and that not working out so well for him. For the most part, I think we had to hold off on the dying part for a certain length of time and, in the interests of brevity, we all managed to succeed on that score. Yay us!

Lucky the Parrot would just like to add here that while I can't recall a single thing that involved the stupid bird, he did come through everything without so much as a feather out of place. So hurrah for him too.

Jonathon never met an itch he didn't want to scratch, so he tried to do a mystic scan/investigation after sharing with the crew that there was something magickal inside. Since magic could possibly mean treasure, I feel safe in saying that this wasn't just Jon's naive idea because he was almost certainly ordered by the Captain to do this and so be the cause of any sort of trouble that may have followed.

He determined that there was mostly the remains of what was a human being once inside the briney chemical confines of the barrel, but was now mostly a floating head, kept alive by some arcane rite of the evil (yes we'll just go there) Illithids. They took certain humanses from our side of the portal and did this to them to gain far-see powers to foretell events and so on so on so forth. It should come as no surprise that Jonathin subsequently made an attempt to communicate with the head and bonded with him spiritually.

This is what I remember though this may be entirely fiction and just wishful thinking on my part. But the magick doth wane in the illicit land of the Illithid's, and they doth need to replenish their failing stock with people from whatever our world is called, probably the more sensitive sorts as well as the bigger breasted of the women. The insidious plan of the Illithids must be stopped!

So while a battle rages on the seas with everyone fighting against the much more powerful illithid ship, the Broken Wind, which has seemingly materialized out of nowhere along with this uncomfortable scene shift that the lowly scribe is too lazy to write any proper prose to relate in any proper sense (why bother when the brilliant Rhoda Book will polish it up?), in this finest of run-on sentences, the mage Jon (who's not big on offensive spells anyway) sought to free the Floating Head Guy from the bondage and cruelty of continued existence by removing the protective spells that maintained his half life while also chanting the Ritual of Ye Soule Passage to allow him safe journey to the fate that doth await us all! C'mon, he already had a good head start.

Meanwhile(!!!), there were a lot of cannonballs with hellfire chasers and whatever the badguys were tossing around (lightning, I just had a flash that it was lightning) and somehow we got the upper hand??? I am guessing the brilliant strategies and cool Captain Kirk-like daring of Barend van der Meer, along with the fiery booms of maniac Lady Morgan, as well as the chill wind skilz of Porter Drayke were more than sufficient to win the battle against the Broken Wind and its puny mystic masters.

I seem to recall Lady Morgan swinging on board the other ship which was already on fire? Weren't there a lot of living-dead crewmen that got killed for good at some point so there were only a couple guys left on the ship. Whatever. Fearless Lady Morgan just can't let a ship get away that isn't entirely scuttled by fire and soon to go down to sweet Davy Jones' Locker. So yeah, she got on board, stole some stuff (wasn't there a journal?), made it impossible to put out the fire, and got away. Meanwhile, the Big Mage Captain Illithid guy also (I think) managed to get away??? It's not my fault...Greg refused to offer any help.

Anyway, it's so hard to remember these sorts of things when my character was busy saving a lost soul from a living hell.

I'm pretty sure we went back to Strathmore and if memory serves despite these oh so many years of mental decline, Jonathon had an encounter with his Father who is not his Father but really is his Father who said, "Son, I ate your Father! I'm sorry but that's how this works. However, I consumed his life before you were born so, I mean, technically, that makes me your real Father, son, and why you have these spiffy but (according to your crewmates) useless mystic powers! That is your Illithid heritage! We sought the domination of your world but now we are so weakened by overuse of magic according to Thee Laws of Niven, that we are too weak to accomplish much, and there be only a few of us on this side. Still, we have the time and energy to hunt renegades like me and abomination hybrids like you. Stay awayyyyyyy from the Illithids, Jonathon my son, my son, for they will be your dooooooooom."

More or less.

So that is what I got. Probably some sort of other business was done in Strathmore by the totally respected other guys.
