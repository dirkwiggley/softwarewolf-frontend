The episode began with the traditional pre opening-credits action sequence where, in this case, someone noticed something glistening in the water and when Nicholas went over the side and into the deep to investigate…and then with steadily-building, ominous music and a flurry of bubbles underwater...he was never seen again. Cue opening stock footage.

Capt. Barend holds Jonathon by the ankles over the side, but relax, it is just so the young mage witch can retrieve some bit of metal stuck into the side of the ship. It turns out to be a tine from a trident, which (after some consultation with those who have sea-legend skills) is considered to be of Merman-make. So our heroes put two and two together (plus maybe a little advanced algebra) to determine that Nicholas was overwhelmed underwater but a veritable horde of Mermen because otherwise there is no logical explanation as to how he could be beaten.

However, without Nicholas’s water-witch powers, the crew has no way of adventuring underwater. Not to mention that in consideration of how most of the crew thought “legends and myths” was a perfectly fine knowledge skill for knowing anything about Mer-People, there is no one who has a clue where they might even be out there under the deep blue sea.

Clearly, this is a time for bold action. It is quickly determined that there is a Marehardian Trading Post on a small island thataway some direction near along the coast line, which is the most local place to be able to gain information regarding Mermen. This sudden plan-of-action is held up by two nail-bitingly important discussions.

The first is above-game. Hey, this coast seems to go on forever? Are we in Elicya still? Some people had heard that this whole southern coastline is technically Elicya. Is this still a southern coastline? It seems/appears to be getting bigger? Is there a map? Yes, our characters have a map but I think it is a magic map that keeps changing. Is this the Land of Imagination where all our dreams can come true if we just believe hard enough? Oh, the GM is working on it and we should knock it off if we know what is good for us?? Moving onward to important discussion point #2.

This was mostly in-character and was mostly ranged around the pulse-pounding intrigue of, hey, who is in charge here anyway on this good boat of ours, The Wicked Hart? “Oh, that is easy,” pipes in Jonathon. “The Captain is our good Capt. Barend who is the biggest and even most sea-worthy of us all!”

“Not so fast,” says Porter Drayke, the pilot, “For I wish to argue semantics that when I am at the wheel and something other than just normal sailing is happening, then I am the Captain and what I say goes!”

This takes just about as long as one might think. But moving to a swift summation, there will actually be no exchanging the Captain’s hat in any situation. Decisions that involve the boat’s movement in the water , it is decided, are naturally to be determined by the Pilot. If he says, “Trim those sails!” then those sails Will Be Trimmed, by Gar! Manning the wheel, manning the tiller, telling people what to do to expedite Mr. Porter Drayke’s operation of the Wicked Hart in any kind of situation are ultimately up to the fastidious demands of Mr. Porter Drayke! Should he require tea swiftly to be brought to him, then so it must be. He will brook no insolence on these matters!!

In everything else, Capt. Barend is the Captain and makes all the other Captain-like decisions. Of course reasonable respect is given to every sailor and/or passenger on the skiff, in that the crew is a small and humble crew and everyone has an opinion which Capt. Barend understands and considers thoughtfully, even when it is wrong and also in those other circumstances when it is very wrong.

Onward they sail to the Marehardian Trading Post!

Things do not go well. The War of Conflict between Casteel and just about the rest of the world, has reached the Great Shores of Mystery upon which The Crew has been sailing. On twin quests of their own, the Casteelians have taken over the Marehardian Trading Post and killed every one of the Marehardians. While that is unfortunate and unsportsmanlike, equally criminal is how The Crew walked all the way up into the Trading Post suspecting nothing was at all suspicious. The Post was, after all, unlawfully flying the Marehard flag and wearing appropriate uniforms, and it’s not easy to see blood stains from a distance.
