The action begins in the Port City of Gullsaim, a small city and port stop that still does reasonable business.  It was crippled some years ago by the burning of the rum distillery, but the family is trying to rebuild from that.  A collection of PC's gravitated to the Gullsaim to seek their fortunes or at least passage off this last-stop-on-the-way-to-nowhere place.  The Furious Beaver is in dock, a smaller ship usually sailing with the Brig, "The Dark And Stormy Night."  Was it in town too?  The GM is stingy with the opening fun-facts sheets.  One thing is for sure, something happened recently that has them a little low on crewmen.

Another thing about Gullsaim, they have a lot of seagulls.  This may be from a curse caused by some kind of sea-demon (more later). They are everywhere, flying over everything, and they sometimes do what birds do at points during the day while flying...which is unfortunate for those beneath them.  Kids in town are +2 with thrown stones against small, flying targets and all of the town's folk have developed a kind of sixth sense for dodging out of the way without actually looking up to be sure.  Nevertheless, everyone puts up with the unpleasant stains on their clothes and hats, and everyone in town wears hats.

For the most part, it is a peaceful town with people following their own interests.  Characters actually doing anything special that I remember include Lady Morgan getting a job with the local blacksmith/tinkerer and working on mending a damaged flintlock pistol that is the treasured gun of Capt. Broadus of the Sloop Dog (although the boss didn't make that clear at the time).  The repair was beyond his skills, but he offered the fair price of half the commission and free use of his tools and workshop to get it done.  The only wrinkle was that she would promise that her participation would remain a secret between the two of them.

Thomas Leery and Hellcat were enjoying themselves by luring townsfolk into a back room or alley via the raising of Hellcat's skirt, and then bang-em-on-the-head to increase the ship's crew.  Their quota was finished before dinner.

Most everyone else was doing stevedore work, helping to unload the ships' cargo onto the port and in to carts where it was transported into the local warehouses.  There was a moment of severe moral deliberation for Johnston, but ultimately he decided that it was a little too risky to steal a crate or two in broad daylight with so many people watching.

By dinner time, everyone was heading towards the Awl Inn which had recently acquired a new cook named Jonathon who actually knows his spices and sauces, is keen on how to prepare vegetables so they have flavor, and is also good with meats and the local favorite, fresh "squab."

Eventually a nighttime ruckus breaks out to draw the disparate characters together.  It would appear that the rest of the pirates did not feel adequately bolstered by the offerings procured by Thomas and Hellcat and decided to raid the city for willing (unconscious) volunteers to join in on a almost rat-free ship for sailing adventure.  There is a lot of fighting going on, most of it confined to fisticuffs or clubs but a few swords do get drawn.  

Johnston and Jack were the first victims, caught off guard into some melee close to the edge of town while they were probably helping some poor old lady get home safely with her groceries.  Barend, Nicholas, and Doc Smythe came to their rescue in what can only be described as overkill.  Nicholas, at some point may and/or may not have demonstrated some ability to conjure up a frozen icicle and cause it to fly towards a kidnapping pirate...but who can really know about these things in the dead of night.

Jonathon stepped out of the back of the Inn, a dishtowel and now-clean dish in his hands to see what all the noise was about as the town is not usually this loud at night.  There was some sort of clamor going on down the way in an alley space where Hellcat was confronted by the Bosun of her own ship, who apparently was the brother of a sailor she had struck down in the manhood for daring to lay drunken and lecherous hands upon her.  The Bosun had bided his time until now where, apparently all the signs pointed to success as his dice did not fail at any time to put the dread-but-lovely Pirate Hellcat at his ruthless mercy.  Fortunately, Thomas was still around to protect her and forestall the Bosun's retribution until subsequent aid could arrive.

By then, young Jon was sore beset by a ridiculous three-brigand-force of his own who were halfway-to-glory in stuffing him in a big sack and carrying him off.  Fortunately, the lad has no sense of pride in considering himself a fighter and was quick to shout out loudly for help.  His desperate struggles also allowed him to avoid getting clobbered over the head with a belaying pin for several moments until help arrived.  Big Mr. Van der Meer showed off his patented, wagon-cart battle technique by pushing the handy thing down the street to scatter the pirates.  Once freed, Jonathon (who distinctly has not had a bit of pigeon poo upon him throughout his entire stay in town), squawked in a familiar enough tone to cause overhead gulls to get mad as wet hens at the remaining pirate who was lingering too close to the cook for comfort, and sent him running off.

Barend next got into it with the Bosun and with a superior display of strength, caused the man to flee in terror for his continued livelihood.  The rest of the fracas broke down shortly thereafter.

Sensing that they may have already over-stayed their welcome in town (if for no other reason than the Port-Side Cannon being fired at them), the two ships (if two ships there were) beat a hasty retreat out on to the open seas.

Come the next day, the Sloop Dog sailed into port with its handsome Captain Broadus and his lovely First-Mate, Mavis Beacon.  For pirates, this is an amiable of bunch of decent enough harties with a passion for fine food and drink, pleasant conversations, and good times with the locals.  The Captain put up a notice offering a tidy reward to anyone who could give him reliable information regarding the scurrilous sea-demon known as Davy Jones, and the whereabouts of his great treasure for having plundered the seas for two or more score of years.  This got everyone's interests up to go about how-to-find-out information regarding this quest for a variety of reasons.  

First off, there is apparently a hand-written journal which tells the history of Gullsaim in atrociously bad poetry and an especially difficult scrawl.  This would eventually lead to all hands on deck in a plan involving the further firing of the cannon, a couple more well- timed disturbances, and more of a to-do with after-hours violence in the streets.  The genius of the plan was its simplicity.  It was all clever cover to allow the daring Johnston to sneak into the Governor's mansion and swipe the book off the upper shelf, replacing it for a time with a similar sized book to evade immediate detection.  The reading of the tome was put into the capable hands of Nicholas, who almost went blind trying to decipher the seemingly crippled hand and and frankly disturbing prose style of the author to make heads or tails of the history.

Meanwhile, Jonathan, being a lad of limited experience and not quite yet so acquainted with the others that he knew what they were up to, tried the more direct approach of simply asking questions.  He did, however, employ his special set of skills to put more attention than usual to the food preparation for the Captain and the others at his table and then asked the chatty Captain about Davy Jones.  This subsequently led him, after hours, to take a fresh apple pie out to visit the old coot, Shifty, who keeps a shack on the beach, and so to pick his brains for what stories he might tell.

To keep things simple, we will combine knowledge gained as the party more fully came together around this point.  Davy Jones once resided in the area as some sort of being of mostly benign intent.  Something then happened to him whereupon he became a kind of demon-of-the-sea who sometimes sent ships to their doom beneath the waves.  He once resided in a local sea cave, but subsequently moved out to the deeper seas and some other, secret, hidden place.  In his original form, he was especially fond of the local Heather, and come the morning, Jonathon and Hellcat (and possibly Thomas) headed up into the hills to gather some of that for a conjuration that Nicholas thought would be helpful.

By the next nightfall, Nicholas had finished with his work with the book (which was then returned to its place of honor by Johnston without all the to-do that was considered so necessary the night before.  Following that, a consensus was reached that a sojourn back up into the hills was necessary to learn the origin of Davy Jones.  Jonathon had brewed up some sort of potion in a small pot which when simmered to perfection would lead the way to a place sympathetic to the once-upon-a-time creature where they might learn what was what.  Young Jon provided a quite convincing argument that quests into the unknown involving mysterious spirits and creatures was best done at night when said spirits were prone to being more lively.  Sure, there might be increased danger, but to young Jon's experience, staying respectful and with proper precaution (he pointed towards his little pot) should see them through safely.  The fact that there were also nine of them and all reasonably capable undoubtedly helped to sell the midnight ramble.

The crew headed up into the hills with torches and a now silvery-glowing, steamy pot.  It was a healthy stretch of the legs until they were entirely out of sight of the city or sea, and then people started to notice flickering lights.  Most gave it little mind except Hellcat and Thomas, who thought it was a pretty display and worth wandering, trance-like, to investigate without telling anyone.  While the darkness made it hard to notice at first, their absence soon gained everyone's attention.  Both of the wizard's know about will-o'-the-wisps who are either simple light trick phenomenon and nothing of any matter, or sometimes actual creatures who reside in these kinds of nature-strong places and how you shouldn't follow them unless you either don't mind having a little mischief done to you, or feel like becoming a part of some kind of destiny.  Briefly, it was considered that the two of them might still be in cahoots with their pirate band and planning an ambush, but then it was considered how that would have to be an awfully big ambush just for Barend alone.  So having bonded somewhat already as a group of friends, it was decided that they would head off to rescue them.

Jonathon had to stay behind on the trail since if he stepped off the pathway-of-his-brew, it would go out and fail and they would be stuck for another night if not more as this sort of augury sometimes gets ornery if you do it wrong the first time.  I think Jack stayed with him while the others went in search of Thomas and Hellcat.  They found them and the little faeries who lured them up there, and Nicholas mainly spoke to them and heard them tell of their old friend who had become Davy Jones after he got swallowed by a great big shark while gliding on the wind a little too close to the water.  Although considering what turned to be the actual size of the shark-monster, it would be unfair to accuse young Davy of being at all careless.  Nick made a promise to the wind creatures to help restore their lost friend.

The party rejoined, they hiked up the rest of the way to the high part of the mountain, which commanded a spectacular view of the countryside and sea.  This was the place with the most aromatic Heather where the wind spirit once preferred to dwell.  Jonathon then began a longer chant, repeating a lengthy phrase quite a number of times, before finally raising up his silvery-glowing pot, causing a beam of pale light to lance out from it in the deep night sky and out across the sea to a small island quite a few miles out from shore.  None of them probably knew about its existence before, but it turned out to be marked on the local map they have.

That done, they head back down to Gullsaim, for what's left of a night's sleep.  Come the morning where some rise early to prepare breakfast with the kitchen staff for a whole lot of people, others get up later to eat and then go about procuring a boat big enough to carry them all across to the island.

This gets done too.  Upon landing, the crew sees that there is a hut set up center on the little islet and from it emerges a lady long-dead but still walking around like she don't mind.  In her unwholesome non-life being, she summons up fierce water-elementals who grab and hurl two of the crew out to sea, Barend and Johnston, who are then assaulted by sea creatures, the first being a gigantic fifty-foot or so eel against Barend, and a sea-troll against poor Johnston.  Meanwhile, there is the lich-witch and the two water elementals who are more dedicated to fighting than just a fast grab-and-throw.  It is an epic struggle, with a few good points worth relating.  Suffice that most of the crew are good enough fighters, bold and uncommonly brave; in fact so much so that they do not lose their courage even in the face of terrifying monsters.  Nicholas' own magic involves an actual offensive spell or two, which he makes good use of.  Jonathon is not so gifted but is at least able to protect himself enough from elemental forces that they will at least think of attacking someone else first, and then oblige by allowing him the opportunity to get out of the way of their watery fists.

Barend actually slew his giant eel menace by himself and pretty quickly, which must have been quite a sight to see if anyone actually saw it since it was all underwater.  The Sea Troll, however, proved to be made of sterner stuff.  The Dead Lady was more than a little dangerous too, what with her fell spells and command of the Elementals.  Having landed on the island close to dawn, when the rising sun's bright light would not shine kindly upon this darksome scene, Jonathon tried another chant, hoping the elementals would either ignore him or continue to fail to grasp him.  His plan was to feel the cleansing might of purest nature coming up west-side and make use of it.  This took a while, but eventually he caused an empowering to those fighting the undead witch, which made them mightier against her.

Thomas Leery struck the final blow against the witch, which dissolved her water elementals, which freed the others to help Barend, Jack, the Doc, and Johnston against the Sea Troll who refused to say die.  Most of the party can swim very well, which of for those who grew in a forest with, at best, a small river-fed pond.  Together they all killed it and then brought it out of the water so that it could have its head cut off and then at least burned enough so that it would stay dead.  There was an unfortunate lack of combustible fuel on the island.

Inside the shack was a journal of someone named Claire the Witch and a whole mess of increments and ingredients for not only wholesome witchcraft, but dark and terrible necromantic rituals.  Nicholas and Jonathon set to the reading of the journal, and it spoke of Claire, who was a witch, and had married into a wealthy merchant-trade family.  But then a series of hard times came upon them to wipe out their fortunes and kill her husband and other sad things.  Claire had set herself the quest of restoring the fortune by finding the treasure of the Sea Demon, Davy Jones.  She had tracked her mission to this place but needed to use a necromantic spell to speak with the dead, specifically someone or thing named Argus, whose corpse was apparently at the bottom of the sea, a little off the island's shore (there was a handy little drawn map). From his lips, or whatever, Claire hoped to learn what she would need to find the demon's lair.  But the sad tale tells of a sickness coming upon her before she could manage this.  Possibly, the sickness might have been brought about or fueled in some manner by the necromantic arts she was studying to learn, though she does not make the nature of the illness clear.

About midway through, Jonathon asked to skip to the end to confirm his feeling that twice-dead Claire was outside, where she fell off the porch in response to Thomas's righteous blade.  The lad excused himself to allow Nicholas to finish the study and stepped outside to chant over the corpse to set the spirit of the lost Witch free while there was still time.

By then, Barend and others had also hauled the giant sea eel to land with the thought that it might be good eating and worth selling back in Gullsaim.  And indeed while it was edible and tasty too, there was no way the crew could haul that huge hulk back to the mainland with their little boat.  But they feasted well that morning on cooked eel.  Over breakfast, Nicholas said how he would fulfill the witch's scheme to learn the necromantic rite to speak with Argus below the sea.  That it was below the sea was not a problem since his own powers would allow him to breath down there, and he could so gift most of the crew as well to join him.  Likewise, he could create light below the water and just knew all sorts of other clever things about the sea. 

Down there, it was discovered that Argus was a hundred-foot long shark that was dead as could be all over the seabed but yet somehow still looking healthy and fleshy for an unmoving, dead 100-foot long shark.  It is safe to say how that was an unexpected development.  Back on dry land, Nicholas was again insisting on doing this, and Jonathon is dead-set against it, saying how necromancy is wicked and corrupting like what happened to Claire the Witch.  He suggested that finding Davy Jones for Capt. Broadus or even the treasure all by itself was not worth the risk to Nicholas's good nature and very soul.  Nicholas said how he wouldn't do it for the treasure but to help the wind spirits regain their lost friend.  Jonathon then said how he would help him do that and working together (and both of them not sickly) should be able protect each other from the dark touch of the magic while performing this good deed.  

How the others felt about the disregarding of treasure did not come up in conversation.  It is possible that the whole crew's easy acceptance of all the monsters and magical conjurations was simply a mask of actual and deep-felt shock that put them of a mind that they really didn't want to think too much of precisely what was going on.

Six or Seven of them went down below the sea again and the ritual was made.  Jonathon mostly kept up the chant while Nicholas and maybe others actually spoke to the now moaning and ever-so-slightly moving giant shark.  It was, to say the least, the sort of thing you just don't see every day.  Turned out this was the shark that got the wind spirit in his mouth and tried to swallow.  The wind spirit did not die inside but stayed alive and sought forever to escape.  The shark ate other things in its swimming and sharking ways, which sometimes included Men.  The Wind Spirit spoke to the dead dinners that the shark enjoyed, and over years of captivity in the sea-monster's belly, the spirit was gradually corrupted into becoming a new sort of creature, which would come to be known as the Sea Demon, Davy Jones.  

The eruption of said creature from the shark doomed Argus to a living death, stuck down on the seabed, dying mostly, but not enough so that he could escape the hellish non-live of unmoving, unthinking, immobilized, eternal-hunger fate that possibly even a hundred-foot long monster-shark didn't deserve.  It was, at least, a creature of nature (freakish perhaps, but still nature) that had given birth to a demon and was now accursed to continuous, tormented existence.

An agreement was made that they would try to set free its spirit so that its body would finally die, but before that (which the two "wizards" would still have done regardless), Argus would tell them what he knew about Davy Jones, and where his new abode was so that he could be found and possibly destroyed so that the wind spirit (or whatever was left of it) could be free as well.

Argus described a sargasso that the more sailor-oriented figured they could find and then the chanting turned into a requiem for the dead.  There was every feeling of the freeing of a spirit to the great beyond of giant sharks, but the body would still be a while in its final up-and-dying like it should.  As no one was any kind of expert at this precise sort of thing, it was figured to be the best that they could do.

Back upside, the crew took a rest, had a lunch, and then back in the boat to head back to Gullsaim.  Once on dry land, everyone wanted a good rest and hot meal, and Jonathon also suggested another trip up into the hills would be necessary because the spirits were going to have to share with the crew the true name of the wind spirit that became Davy Jones if they were going to be able to set it free.  Likewise, the sailing crew would have to procure a bigger boat.
