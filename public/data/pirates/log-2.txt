Things have mostly returned to normal in Gullsaim. The heroes (as they like to think of themselves) are just hanging around, waiting for the next move which allegedly involves witchcraft of one sort or another from on of them witches or the other. But only one of them is doing research while the other is either in the kitchen or out gathering herbs and such for the kitchen.

A new individual saunters into town, Porter Butler who is a Ship’s Pilot in search of a ship needing piloting. He’s good at his job and, while he doesn’t list it on his resume, has special skills at calling and guiding the winds over the seas to keep his ship’s sails filled. How he has come to be presently unemployed is anyone’s guess.

Gullsaim has an annual event of a fishing contest with first prize going to him what catches the biggest fish. No one has won this contest for years and years because the bad luck that generally haunts the area becomes So Bad for this one day that no one ever even catches a single fish. Interestingly, the town has added to the purse every year, in lieu of a winner, so that the prize money is enough to buy a handsome skiff made by Leaky Windsor. That is a fr’ instance allocation-of-the-funds should said contest be won by a member of the party (that is hoping to some day be called “the crew”). The general line of thinking is that Nicholas, with his sea-talents, should be a shoo-in for catching a simple, single fish. So that is the explanation of why no one else is carving any sort of special fishing pool from so-called sacred wood, or putting any effort into digging up the wriggliest of worms. The day of the contest is a few days away at the start of things.

Nicholas has been handling the heavy lifting of necromantic research regarding how to go about scrying out the location of the precise coral reef that will lead the party further on in their quest to find Mr. Davy Jones. Most of the party is likely convinced there is great treasure to be had in the finding. Nicholas and Jonathon are primarily motivated by the idea of freeing the spirit of the wind elemental that was drawn into the creation of the Sea-Demon in the first place.

Jonathon gets easily unnerved regarding necromancy, and is fine with focusing on his kitchen duties that keep everyone well fed, and stepping up for witchy-council only when needed.

Meanwhile, news in town is that the Waft Brothers, Bob and Wayde, have gone missing from their own daily fishing job, having set out the morning before and still not returned. The worst is feared. They are both capable sailors and so no one can imagine what might have happened to them. Everyone heading out on to the water is encouraged to keep a look out as just a good-neighborly thing.

The party has rented a boat to go out on that water after Nicholas announces that they can now find the coral riff with the aid of dead hand (or more precisely, a hand from a dead body) to point the way. It seemed appropriate to use the hand from the dead witch, Claire, from that little island, as it was easy to fetch and mostly on the way. It was a handy appropriation. Further sailing then took the boat further out to sea, led by the pointed finger of destiny to a coral-laden area where they anchored the boat for a spell. That spell being another one of Nicholas’s which he cast only on himself and Jonathon so that they could breath and swim better underwater for what was likely to be a longer time than before.

Down under, the searched about, guided by the hand, towards a small-sized citadel made of coral and shells and glistening in the faint sunlight that shimmered infrequently down to the ocean floor. It was a pretty thing and so disarming to those inclined to imagine pretty things as sweet and harmless. Fortunately, Nicholas was still more on his guard than wide-eyed Jonathon as they passed through the tall entry-way.

Inside they encountered a one time sea-captain and scholar named William Joseph McAllister of the good ship, Bobbie Gentry. Billy Joe was as dead as should be imagined, after having committed suicide many years before (for personal reasons he don’t wanna talk about) by jumping off the Tallahachie Bridge. How he came to this colorful keep within the Sargasso, let alone still be walking around and talking to people, is a story to be told at another time. Suffice, apparently, that sorta thing do happen.

Billy is reportedly a minion of Davey Jones, and a cooperative one. There is, again, likely a pretty good yarn about that too, almost certainly associated with the first one. Billy is amicable and don’t mind telling at least certain tales to those who amble on down to the lower depths for an uninvited visit. He pretty well assumes that anyone doing so of their own free will is interested in Davey and how to go about finding him. So he sets to telling more about how Davey came to be Davey, and it is fair warning that even at the bottom of the sea, this next part is a little hard to swallow.
