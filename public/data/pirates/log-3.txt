This might/will/better-be/was-not shorter than usual because we have no time, man! Not to mention that we barely remember anything because we are soooo old. Only the stuff that will possibly/almost-certainly be redacted (by request of the two party "witches") really sticks in our brains.

Having successfully defeated/killed a band of murderous orcs, led by an impressive battle-shaman and winning practically bupkis in victory swag (sticklers will recall the Serpent Staff pried from the cold, dead fingers of the Shaman, but the reward value of said item might be afflicted by the high-moral dudgeon of certain people), our impoverished crew sails up to the coastal city of TimBukDu for food and lodging and further provisioning.

Being a young man of fine moral fiber, Jonathon sets out to find gainful employment in town. His cause is further taken in tow by Lady Morgan, who also prefers to enjoy 3 squares a day plus a decent bed. There is rumor that the crew actually has enough adequate funds to make this sort of thing unnecessary, but no one shares that news with industrious Jon.

The rest of the crew separates for their own different duties and pleasures. Capt. Barend van der Meer (with Alex along) goes about asserting his position as Captain (status mostly granted by Jonathon just starting to call him that out of politeness) by making proper shipping business arrangements. This involves mainly purchasing inexpensive goods from this town, which will then be sold at a higher price in another town to make a handsome profit. This is what our daring crew calls “Piracy on the High Seas.” He also picked up some guns, powder, and shot for the ship from the local Blacksmith/Gunsmith, Justin Case.

I have to confess that even I’ve used that name before in a game.

Alex did suggest that profits could be increased by leaving out the tedious, “buying the goods” part of the process. This more advanced form of finance has been set aside for the present.

We are pretty sure that the rest of the party was primarily interested in drinking, gambling, and general carousing. This important business was mostly accomplished at Nancy’s Harbor Café, often involving Nancy or one of her very fine friends. Ship’s Pilot, Nicholas, did well enough at gambling to win good coin away from local, professional cheater-at-cards, Jack Potts. Hellcat even managed to quaff a few tankards of ale without emasculating anyone.

Nancy’s was not a place that either Lady Morgan nor Jonathon made a thought to stop at in their own quest as Nancy’s Harbor Café smells awful even from the other side of the street. Very disreputable, Jon needs a bath.

That duo went further into town to discover the Nobody Inn, with Morgan playing spokesman to the manager, Frank Sanbeens, regarding the services of the high quality chef she was presenting to him, young Jonathon. Subsequently, Jon had very little time to become acquainted with the kitchen to turn out something delicious for lunch, or get sacked. The lad had to focus his chi and bear down on his soufflé, drawing upon some special spices and mushrooms to tingle the tastebuds of Mr. Sanbeens, but ultimately prevailed. His own small room, fair pay, and some manner of discount for Lady Morgan’s own, nicer room were settled quickly enough.

To wrap up the cuisine contest, Sue Fley, the rival cook at the Nobody Inn, wound up getting along fine with pleasant Jon, and they shared recipes and giggled and made excellent dining a matter of course at the always ready-for-you Nobody Inn. Just send a runner for reservations today.

Dusty Broom works at the N.I. too, but to no particular incident in my recollection.

Two citizens of interest in town are two best-selling authors (hundreds of copies!), Rhoda Book, who penned the thriller, “Orcs of the Western Sea, Vol. 1,” and is currently working on the riveting sequel cleverly titled, “Orcs of the Western Sea, Vol. 2.” Paige Turner her live-in companion/sister shares their charming family home with several cats and a lovely garden. Paige is a writer of fiction, best known for the Dashing Pirate Fabio series.

Lady Morgan made the opportunity to meet with the sisters, have tea, and, as ladies do, discuss orcs. We are pretty sure that Nicholas accompanied her on that excursion. Recent escapades came up in conversation and Lady M won herself a sort of battle-correspondent stipend in regards reports of potential upcoming events and adventures. It was imagined that these would provide grist for the mill of Rhoda’s exhaustive tome on the always fascinating, fan-pleasing subject of orcs.

At some time during the few days spent in town, Porter wandered into Knob Park, near town center and noticed an odd feeling and strange resonance about the place. Sadly, Nate is a selfish keeper-of-secrets and no-share-to-me-that’s-fair kind of pig-boy; and so he received zero points later on when Nicholas noticed the same thing and brought Jonathon by and together they witched out most of the mystery of the place.
